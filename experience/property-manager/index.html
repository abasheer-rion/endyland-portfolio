Ran a 23-unit rental property and raised its income 60% in 4 months.

The problem

Running a rental property is mostly small jobs that never stop: leases to write, rent to chase, complaints to settle, repairs to arrange. The risk for an owner is that income stalls or tenants leave.

I took over the daily operations of a 23-unit property in December 2024, and worked on the two numbers an owner feels most: how much rent comes in, and how many tenants stay.

What I did

I priced from data instead of by feel. I built Excel dashboards and Python scripts that track rent automatically, which saved 20+ hours a month.

I handled lease paperwork, tenant relations and complaints, and coordinated maintenance before small problems became big ones. I signed 12+ new leases.

  • 60%more rental income in 4 months
  • 90%of tenants kept
  • $200K+rent collected
  • 12+new leases signed

How it works

One loop, run every month: the data sets the price, the leases and collection follow, and good service keeps tenants in place.

Results

Rental income rose 60% in four months, and nine in ten tenants stayed.

What I learned

  • Numbers beat instinct. Once rent and pricing were on a dashboard, the right move was usually plain.
  • Keeping a tenant is cheaper than finding one. Answering complaints early did more for income than any price change.
