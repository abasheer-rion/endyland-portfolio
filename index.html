About

I started in network engineering. I spent two summers at Verizon in Richardson, Texas, as an intern in software and automation. I found 200+ live voice-network devices with no backups and automated the fix. I built the Carrier Trunk Provisioning Tool on my own: the record system and API behind a $70M-a-year revenue line. Moving apps off VMware saves $480K a year. The Verizon work

At UT Dallas I am studying electrical engineering on the Fast Track BS/MS, and my interest moved to antennas and RF. In the RIDE program, a team of three built a 5.8 GHz array that finds where a signal comes from, then steers its beam toward it, along with a Butler matrix that steers with fixed phase shifts. I like the parts of a product most people never see: the servers, the tunnels, the pipes between things. The direction finder

I care about things that work when nobody is watching.

Then startups. I cofounded Argus, an app for running a convenience store that started with AI cameras that spot theft. I led engineering and owned product strategy, pricing and delivery. Argus ran in 10+ stores, reached 92% model accuracy and raised a first round at a $1M valuation. It ended in 2026. Alongside it I worked as an automation engineer at RION Solutions, the agency that is Argus’s parent company. The Argus story

In November 2026 I won HackUTD with Safe Home, a home black box that keeps reporting when Wi-Fi and cell go down, with one map for first responders. I led product, design and integration for a team of four over two days. How Safe Home works
